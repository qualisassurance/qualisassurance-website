What loading supervision catches

The losses that happen after the goods pass inspection

An inspection proves the furniture was right. Loading supervision proves that furniture — in that quantity, in that condition — is what actually went into the container.

LOAD-01 · QUANTITY

Short shipment

Cartons counted against the packing list rather than your invoice. Orders arrive 5–20% short, discovered at destination when the supplier has already been paid.

CRITICAL
LOAD-02 · SUBSTITUTION

Different goods to the ones inspected

Approved samples pass, then B-grade or unapproved SKUs go into the container. Without a witness at stuffing, nobody can prove which stock was loaded.

CRITICAL
LOAD-03 · CONTAINER

A container that shouldn't be used

Roof pinholes, damp floors, rust or residual odour from previous cargo. Arrives as water damage or contaminated stock and gets argued about for months.

MAJOR
LOAD-04 · STOWAGE

Crushed by the load above it

Heavy case goods stacked on cartons never built to carry them, or loose stow that shifts across six weeks at sea. Damage created entirely at the loading bay.

What our inspector actually does

  • ✓Inspects the empty container first — floor, walls, roof and doors checked for holes, damp, rust and odour before a single carton goes in.
  • ✓Counts every carton against your invoice, not the supplier's packing list, and reconciles SKU by SKU.
  • ✓Checks condition as goods are loaded — carton damage, damp, wrong markings, opened boxes.
  • ✓Supervises the stow — weight distribution, bracing, dunnage, and stops the load if the pattern will cause transit damage.
  • ✓Verifies desiccant and ventilation where the goods or route need it — the main defence against mould on long sea legs.
  • ✓Records and photographs the seal against the container number, so any tampering downstream is provable.
  • ✓Sends live photos on WhatsApp while it happens, so you can make a call before the doors close.

Questions

Container loading supervision — FAQ

How is this different from a pre-shipment inspection?

A pre-shipment inspection checks the quality of the goods, usually at around 80% packed. Loading supervision checks that those goods, in the right quantity and condition, actually go into the container and that it is stowed and sealed properly. They answer different questions — which is why most buyers eventually book both.

Do I need both, or is loading supervision enough on its own?

If you already trust the supplier's quality and your losses are count, damage or transit-related, loading supervision alone is the right spend. If quality is the risk, you need the inspection. For a first order or a supplier with a claims history, book the Protected Container and cover both.

What happens if the inspector finds a problem mid-load?

They stop and call you. You get photographs on WhatsApp while the container is still open, and you decide — restow, swap the container, hold the short-shipped lines, or proceed. That decision window is the entire point of the service.

Can you supervise a consolidated container from several suppliers?

Yes, and on consolidated loads it matters more. One supplier's damp or damaged goods will affect everything sealed in with them. We reconcile each supplier's cartons separately and supervise the combined stow.

How much notice do you need?

Same day is often possible in Jodhpur and Jaipur, 24 hours in Delhi NCR, and 48–72 hours in Saharanpur, Moradabad and Kolkata. Loading dates move constantly in India, so tell us early and we will hold the slot — rescheduling costs you nothing.
