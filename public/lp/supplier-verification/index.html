What verification catches

Everything looks the same on a website

Sourcing platforms, trade fairs and WhatsApp introductions all present the same polished picture. These are the four gaps we find most often between the picture and the premises.

SV-01 · IDENTITY

A trader presenting as a manufacturer

The most common finding by far. A real, registered business — with no machinery, no workforce and no control over the factory that actually builds your order. Quality problems become unfixable because your counterparty cannot fix them.

CRITICAL
SV-02 · PREMISES

An address that isn't a factory

A rented office, a residential unit, or a shed borrowed for the video call. Photographs of a real factory — just not this company's.

CRITICAL
SV-03 · CAPACITY

Capacity that cannot exist

Forty containers a month claimed from a floor and workforce that could produce eight. Your order gets subcontracted out to workshops nobody inspects, and consistency collapses.

MAJOR
SV-04 · STANDING

No export or legal standing

Registrations lapsed, no export history, or a company that has existed for four months. Recoverable if you know before you pay; not if you find out after.

Questions

Supplier verification — FAQ

How is this different from an inspection?

An inspection checks the goods. Verification checks the company — before goods exist and before you have paid. It answers whether this is a real manufacturer, whether they can build your product, and whether they can build it at your volume. Most losses we see on first India orders happen at this stage, not at production.

The supplier sent photos and a video call. Isn't that enough?

Photographs are the easiest thing in this trade to borrow. We regularly find factory images used by companies that don't own them, and a video call shows you whichever corner of whichever building the caller chooses. The whole point of an unannounced visit is that nobody had time to prepare it.

Do you tell the supplier you're coming?

No. An arranged visit shows you an arranged factory — machinery running, floor swept, workers present. We turn up unannounced, which is also why occasionally we get turned away. That refusal is itself a finding, and it goes in the report.

What if the supplier fails?

You have saved a deposit and several months. The report sets out exactly what failed and why, and if you want, we can verify alternatives — as a fixed fee you pay, with no commission from anyone we assess. That firewall is why our shortlist means something.

How long does it take?

Three to five working days for a single supplier in Jodhpur or Jaipur, longer in the other clusters where travel is involved. Bookings are confirmed within two hours with a fixed quote.
