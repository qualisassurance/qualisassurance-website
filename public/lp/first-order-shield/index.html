How it works

Three checkpoints, three different questions

A first order fails in three distinct ways, and a single inspection at the end only catches one of them. Each checkpoint sits where you still have leverage over that particular risk.

CHECKPOINT 01

Supplier verification

BEFORE THE DEPOSIT

Legal entity checked against registries, premises visited unannounced, machinery and workforce assessed, capacity measured against what they claimed.

Question answered: is this a real manufacturer who can build my product at my volume?

CHECKPOINT 02

Production check

MID-RUN, AROUND 30–50%

Moisture readings taken early, joinery and construction checked against spec, finish and materials verified before the whole run is committed.

Question answered: is what they are building actually what I ordered — while it can still be changed?

CHECKPOINT 03

Pre-shipment inspection

AT 80% PACKED

AQL 2.5 sampling to ISO 2859-1, every finding photographed and classified, moisture verified against your destination climate, clear pass or fail.

Question answered: do I release the balance payment, demand rework, or hold the container?

Why first orders specifically

The mistakes you only make once

Experienced importers have already paid for these lessons. On a first order, all four are live at the same time.

FOS-01 · COUNTERPARTY

The supplier isn't a factory

Our most common verification finding: a real, registered trading company with no machinery and no control over whoever actually builds your order. Quality problems become unfixable because your counterparty cannot fix them.

CRITICAL
FOS-02 · TIMBER

Moisture nobody measured

Sheesham shipped above 12% cracks weeks after delivery in a heated European or American home. Caught mid-run it costs the factory a kiln cycle. Caught at your warehouse it costs you the order.

CRITICAL
FOS-03 · SPECIFICATION

The sample and the order diverge

An approved sample built by the owner's best two men, then a production run subcontracted across three workshops. Nobody lied — but nothing matches.

MAJOR
FOS-04 · LEVERAGE

Finding out after you've paid

The structural problem with first orders: by the time goods arrive, deposit and balance are both gone. Every checkpoint above exists to move discovery earlier, while money still gives you leverage.

Questions

First Order Shield — FAQ

Is $649 worth it on a small first order?

Depends on order value. On a $10,000 first order it is roughly 6% — expensive-looking until you compare it against losing the deposit or shipping a container you cannot sell. On a $40,000 order it is under 2% and an easy decision. If your order is genuinely small, book supplier verification alone at $299; that single checkpoint prevents the largest losses.

We found our supplier at a trade fair. Do we still need verification?

Trade fairs prove someone paid for a stand and had samples made. They don't prove who built the samples, whether the company owns a factory, or whether it can produce at your volume. Some of the clearest trader-not-manufacturer findings we have made were companies with well-run exhibition stands.

What happens if the supplier fails the first checkpoint?

You have saved a deposit and several months, and the remaining two checkpoints stay on your account for whichever supplier you choose instead. The report sets out exactly what failed and why. If you want alternatives assessed, that is a separate fixed fee you pay — we take no commission from any factory we assess.

Can we add container loading supervision?

Yes, for $199 as an add-on. Worth it if your risk is count accuracy, transit damage or mould — the loading bay is where all three are decided. Mention it at booking and we will scope it together.

Who actually does the work?

A named inspector, the same person across all three visits, who signs each report and takes the debrief call. Continuity matters here — by checkpoint three they know your product, your supplier, and what went wrong at checkpoint two.

Are you connected to any Indian factory?

No. Qualis is buyer-paid and takes zero commission from factories. Supplier shortlisting is a fixed fee paid by you and firewalled from inspection work. An inspector paid by the party being inspected has no reason to fail anything.
