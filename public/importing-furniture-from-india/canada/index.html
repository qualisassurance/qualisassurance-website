Market guide · Canada

Importing furniture from India to Canada

Canada looks like an extension of the US market until two things catch you out: winters that run drier than anywhere in the continental States, and a French-language labelling regime that has to be built into the product at the factory. Both are cheap to get right in Jodhpur and expensive to retrofit in Montreal.

The physical risk

A Canadian winter is drier than a US one

Central heating running against −20°C outside air pulls indoor humidity to levels a Rajasthan workshop never contemplates. This is the harshest drying environment of any market we ship to — harder even than the northern United States.

WHY CANADA IS THE HARDEST CLIMATE WE SHIP TO

The wood does not just shrink — it shrinks further than in any other market, then partially recovers in a humid summer, then shrinks again the next winter. That repeated cycling loosens joints that a single dry spell would survive. It is the reason construction quality and glue-up matter even more than the headline moisture number.

HOW WE CHECK — multiple pin-meter readings per item, recorded individually and photographed with the meter in frame, failed against the Canadian destination specification rather than a generic figure.

The Canada-specific trap

Labelling is bilingual, and it is built at the factory

Most compliance failures on Canada-bound furniture are not quality problems. They are label problems — the finished goods arrive with English-only or missing labelling, and fixing that after the container lands means re-labelling in a Canadian warehouse at several times the origin cost.

CANADA — LABELLING & COMPOSITE WOOD OBLIGATIONS
CANFERFormaldehyde emissions — SOR/2021-148. Canada's composite wood regulation mirrors US TSCA Title VI emission limits for hardwood plywood, particleboard, MDF and thin-MDF. Finished goods containing composite wood must carry a compliance label — and here is the catch: it must be bilingual. A US TSCA Title VI label is accepted only if the French translation is present; otherwise the statement "CANFER compliant / conforme au CANFER" is used.
BILINGUALEnglish and French across the board. Under the Consumer Packaging and Labelling Act, mandatory label information on pre-packaged consumer products must appear in both official languages. This is federal, not just Quebec.
QUEBEC · 96French given no lesser prominence. Quebec's Bill 96 strengthened the Charter of the French Language: French must not be smaller or less prominent than any other language on labels, instructions, warranty cards and promotional inserts. Compliance dates have already passed, with a phase-out for older stock. If any of your volume goes to Quebec, this shapes the label design itself.
CCPSAConsumer product safety. The Canada Consumer Product Safety Act prohibits misleading labelling and sets product-specific requirements. Children's furniture, cribs and cradles carry their own mandatory standards and testing.
ISPM 15Packaging timber. Pallets, crates and dunnage must be heat treated and stamped. The furniture itself, if MDF or plywood, does not need ISPM 15 — but the wood under it does.
WHERE THIS BREAKS — AND WHY IT IS AVOIDABLE

The factory prints the label it always prints: English, or English plus a token French line in smaller type. It clears the US fine and fails Quebec. Because the label is applied during production, it is verifiable during production — we check that the bilingual compliance statement is present, correctly worded and gives French no lesser prominence, before the goods are packed rather than after they are rejected.

Not legal advice. An orientation summary for Canadian furniture importers, not a compliance opinion. Requirements differ by product, material and province, and Quebec's rules are distinct from the federal baseline. Composite wood laminated-product testing provisions phase in over time. Confirm your position with a licensed customs broker and current CBSA, Health Canada and Office québécois de la langue française guidance.
Where Canadian orders go wrong

Four failures on the India–Canada route

RISK 01 · LABELLING

English-only labels

The commonest Canada failure and the cheapest to prevent. A label that satisfies the US fails Quebec, and re-labelling a container in Montreal costs many times what printing it correctly in Jodhpur would have.

RISK 02 · TIMBER

Wet wood into the driest winters

Sheesham shipped above 8% does not survive a prairie winter. The repeated shrink-and-recover cycle across seasons loosens joints that a milder climate would leave intact.

RISK 03 · TRANSIT DAMAGE

A long, cold sea leg

Five to seven weeks to the East Coast, then onward rail across a continent. Packaging built for a shorter journey fails, and Canadian e-commerce return handling is unforgiving.

RISK 04 · SUBSTITUTION

Certified panel swapped mid-run

CANFER mirrors TSCA, so the same failure applies: certified board runs out, cheaper stock goes in, and the compliance label no longer describes the container.

Questions

Canadian importers ask

Can you check bilingual labelling at the factory?

Yes, and it is the single most useful Canada-specific check we do. We confirm the CANFER or bilingual compliance statement is present, correctly worded and — for Quebec-bound stock under Bill 96 — that French is given no lesser prominence than English. Because the label is applied during production, catching an English-only run at inspection saves you re-labelling an entire container in a Canadian warehouse.

Is a US TSCA Title VI label enough for Canada?

Only if it is bilingual. Canada's CANFER regulation mirrors the US emission limits and will accept a TSCA Title VI conformance statement, but only when the French translation is present. Without it, the label needs to carry "CANFER compliant / conforme au CANFER". If your supplier already produces for the US market, this is usually a translation fix rather than a testing one — but it still has to happen at the factory.

What moisture content should I specify for Canada?

6–8% for solid hardwood, the tightest specification on our list, because central heating against a deep-freeze winter takes Canadian interiors drier than anywhere in the continental US. If your volume goes to coastal British Columbia the swing is gentler, so tell us the destination province and we will set it accordingly.

Do I need to worry about Quebec if I ship to Ontario or Alberta?

Federal bilingual labelling under the Consumer Packaging and Labelling Act applies nationwide, so English and French are required regardless of province. Quebec's Bill 96 adds prominence rules on top. Most importers simply build to the Quebec standard for everything — one compliant label is cheaper than two label runs.

How fast can you get to a factory?

Same day in Jodhpur and Jaipur, 24 hours in Delhi NCR, 48–72 hours in Saharanpur, Moradabad and Kolkata. Bookings confirmed within two hours with a fixed quote. India is 9.5 to 13.5 hours ahead of Canadian time zones, so a request sent at the end of your day is usually answered overnight.

Are you connected to any Indian factory or agent?

No. Qualis is buyer-paid and takes zero commission from factories. Supplier shortlisting is a fixed fee paid by you and firewalled from inspection work. An inspector paid by the party being inspected has no reason to fail anything.
