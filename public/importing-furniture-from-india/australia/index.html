The Australian-specific risk

Biosecurity outranks quality here

DAFF assesses every imported consignment for biosecurity risk, and furniture triggers it twice over — timber content and timber packaging. Solid timber, bamboo and rattan can all harbour insects and plant disease, and manufactured boards like MDF and plywood are lower risk but still assessed case by case.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A CONSIGNMENT FAILS — ESCALATING COST
STEP 01Held for inspection on arrival. Storage and demurrage accrue daily while the assessment runs.
STEP 02Treatment ordered. Fumigation or heat treatment onshore, at your cost — and some treatments will mark or damage painted, varnished and coated finishes.
STEP 03Re-packaging. Non-compliant timber packaging has to be replaced before release. You pay for that at the Australian port.
STEP 04Re-export or destruction. Where risk cannot be resolved, the consignment is shipped back at your expense or destroyed. This is the outcome no other market on our list can impose.
THE MISTAKE MOST IMPORTERS MAKE

Telling every supplier to fumigate everything. Requirements vary by species, construction, manufacturing process and country of origin, and the applicable BICON conditions should be checked first — some treatments are not suitable for painted, varnished or coated timber. Blanket fumigation can damage your finish and still leave you non-compliant on the thing that actually mattered.

WHAT WE VERIFY AT ORIGIN — BEFORE THE VESSEL SAILS
ISPM 15Packaging timber marked and compliant. Pallets, crates, dunnage and packing blocks must be heat treated or fumigated and carry the ISPM 15 stamp. We photograph the mark on the actual pallets under your goods — not a certificate for pallets used on someone else's order.
CLEANLINESSBark, soil, seeds, insects, nesting. Contamination is the commonest trigger and the easiest to fix at the loading bay. We inspect the container interior and the goods before stuffing.
TREATMENTCertificates matched to the batch. Fumigation or heat treatment certificates checked against the goods and dates actually in your consignment, not held on file generically.
NATURAL FIBRECane, rattan, bamboo, water hyacinth. Higher biosecurity attention and higher mould risk. Checked for residual damp and pest evidence at the point it is wrapped.
DOCUMENTSPacking declaration consistency. Product description, quantities, weights, consignee and container number must agree across invoice, packing list, bill of lading and treatment certificates. Mismatches alone cause holds.
ONE THING YOU PROBABLY DON'T NEED

Brown marmorated stink bug seasonal measures run September to April and apply to goods manufactured in or shipped from target risk countries — chiefly the United States and much of Europe. India is not among them, so Indian-origin furniture is not generally caught by mandatory BMSB treatment. If a forwarder is quoting you offshore BMSB treatment on a Mundra sailing, ask them to point to the requirement. Vessel routing can still matter, so confirm with your broker.

Beyond biosecurity

Timber legality and consumer law

AUSTRALIA — OTHER OBLIGATIONS ON IMPORTED FURNITURE
ILLEGAL LOGGINGDue diligence on timber legality. Importers of timber and timber products may be required to take steps to establish that the wood carries a low risk of having been illegally logged. Same evidence problem as EUDR and the Lacey Act: species, origin and a documented chain back through the yards.
TOPPLINGToppling furniture information standard. Covered tall and storage furniture requires permanent warning labels, safety information in the instructions, point-of-sale warnings and warnings in online listings. This is a labelling and documentation duty — and labels are applied at the factory, which is where we check them.
AS/NZSMandatory product standards. Certain categories carry mandatory safety standards under Australian Consumer Law — household cots being the clearest example. Confirm which apply to your range before production, not after.
ELECTRICALPowered components and batteries. Furniture with lighting, charging ports, powered mechanisms or button batteries picks up additional requirements. Worth settling before packaging, labels and instructions are signed off.
Not legal advice. An orientation summary for Australian furniture importers, not a compliance opinion. Biosecurity conditions are determined case by case through BICON and depend on species, construction and origin. Confirm your position with a licensed customs broker and current DAFF and ACCC guidance before you ship.
The physical risk

One country, two opposite climates

Perth, Adelaide and inland Australia are dry enough to split Indian hardwood. Brisbane, Sydney and the tropical north are humid enough to mould it. A single national specification produces claims at both ends.

Where Australian orders go wrong

Four failures on the India–Australia route

RISK 01 · BIOSECURITY

Someone else's pallets

The factory holds an ISPM 15 certificate; the pallets under your cartons are unmarked scrap from the yard. Nobody checks at origin because nobody is there, and the hold happens 9,000 km later.

RISK 02 · CONTAMINATION

Bark, soil and insects

Cheap and trivial to fix at the loading bay. Expensive and slow to fix at Port Botany, where treatment can also mark your finish.

RISK 03 · TIMBER

Moisture set to the wrong climate

Stock specified for Sydney sent to Perth splits through summer. Stock specified for Perth sent to Cairns swells and moulds. Both are avoidable by specifying per state.

RISK 04 · CARTON DATA

Volume, not weight

Australian furniture freight is usually charged by volume. Suppliers quote assembled dimensions rather than final packed carton dimensions and gross weights, and your landed cost lands wrong.

Questions

Australian importers ask

Should I just tell my supplier to fumigate everything?

No — this is the most expensive habit we see. Requirements vary by species, construction, manufacturing process and origin, and the applicable BICON conditions should be checked first. Some treatments are not suitable for painted, varnished or coated timber, so blanket fumigation can damage your finish while still leaving you exposed on the thing that actually mattered, usually the packaging.

Can you verify ISPM 15 compliance at the factory?

Yes, and it is the single highest-value check on this route. We photograph the ISPM 15 stamp on the actual pallets and crates going under your goods, rather than accepting a certificate that may relate to different timber. Unmarked packaging is the commonest cause of a hold, and it costs almost nothing to correct while the container is still open.

Do I need BMSB treatment on Indian-origin furniture?

Generally not. Seasonal brown marmorated stink bug measures run September to April and apply to goods manufactured in or shipped from target risk countries, principally the United States and much of Europe. India is not among them. Vessel routing can still bring measures into play, so confirm with your broker — but you should not be paying for offshore BMSB treatment on a straightforward Mundra sailing without a stated reason.

What moisture content should I specify?

Tell us the destination state and we will set it. As a general specification we recommend 9–11%, but Perth, Adelaide and inland destinations warrant drier stock at 8–10%, while Brisbane and the tropical north sit at the other end and need more attention to desiccant and ventilation than to dryness.

How fast can you get to a factory?

Same day in Jodhpur and Jaipur, 24 hours in Delhi NCR, 48–72 hours in Saharanpur, Moradabad and Kolkata. Bookings confirmed within two hours with a fixed quote. Australian eastern time runs four and a half to five and a half hours ahead of us, so a request sent in your morning is answered the same working day.

Are you connected to any Indian factory or agent?

No. Qualis is buyer-paid and takes zero commission from factories. Supplier shortlisting is a fixed fee paid by you and firewalled from inspection work. An inspector paid by the party being inspected has no reason to fail anything.
