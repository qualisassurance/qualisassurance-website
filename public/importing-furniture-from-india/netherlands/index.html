Importing furniture from India to the Netherlands

Rotterdam is where a great deal of Indian furniture enters the European Union — including furniture that is never sold in the Netherlands. That makes Dutch importers the EU operator on paper, carrying liability for onward markets too. Here is what that means, and what actually damages containers on this route.

The Dutch-specific exposure

If it clears in Rotterdam, you are the operator

Under EUDR the party that first places goods on the EU market is the operator, and the operator carries primary legal responsibility. For a Dutch wholesaler importing from India and distributing onward into Germany, Belgium or France, that party is you — for every one of those markets.

WHO CARRIES WHAT — TYPICAL DUTCH DISTRIBUTION CHAIN
ORIGINIndian factory. Supplies documentation on request. No EUDR filing obligation of its own — the burden does not start here.
YOUDutch importer — the operator. First to place goods on the EU market. Files the due diligence statement, holds the evidence, carries the liability.
ONWARDGerman, Belgian, French buyers. Downstream traders. They reference your statement rather than assembling evidence themselves.

WHY THIS MATTERS MORE FOR DUTCH IMPORTERS

A German retailer importing for its own shelves carries its own risk. A Dutch distributor importing for fifteen European customers carries theirs as well — and will be asked for the reference number by every one of them. The evidence file stops being a compliance chore and becomes a commercial document your customers evaluate you on.

ENFORCEMENT — in the Netherlands, timber regulation compliance falls to the NVWA (Nederlandse Voedsel- en Warenautoriteit). Confirm current competent-authority guidance directly, as EUDR implementation arrangements are still settling across member states.

The route risk

Damp is the Dutch problem, not drought

German importers lose furniture to central heating drying it out. The Dutch failure mode is the opposite end of the same physics — a maritime climate, humid warehousing, and a container that spent five weeks sweating on the way to Rotterdam.

THE FAILURE NOBODY PHOTOGRAPHS AT THE FACTORY

Mould does not exist when the container is stuffed. It appears on arrival, which is why it is almost always argued about rather than prevented. The controllable variables are all at origin: moisture at packing, whether the goods were packed warm, ventilation and desiccant in the container, and whether cane or rattan was properly dried before wrapping.

HOW WE CHECK — pin-meter readings per item recorded individually, packing condition assessed before sealing, desiccant and ventilation verified at stuffing, and natural fibre checked for residual damp at the point it is wrapped.

Where Dutch orders go wrong

Four failures we see on this route

RISK 01 · NATURAL FIBRE

Cane, rattan and water hyacinth

Dutch buyers import more natural-fibre furniture than most European markets. Wrapped before it is fully dry, it arrives spotted with mould and is unsellable at full margin — a Kolkata-cluster problem more than a Jodhpur one.

RISK 02 · MIXED CONSIGNMENT

Consolidated containers

Dutch distributors often consolidate several suppliers into one container. One supplier's damp goods will mould the container's contents, not just their own cartons.

RISK 03 · ONWARD SPEC

One spec, several destination climates

Stock that clears in Rotterdam and sells into heated German homes needs the drier German specification, not the Dutch one. Buying to a single average produces claims at both ends.

RISK 04 · DOCUMENTATION

Evidence your customers will audit

As the operator you will be asked for due diligence reference numbers by every downstream buyer. Thin evidence becomes a commercial problem long before it becomes a regulatory one.

Regulatory position

What applies to a Dutch furniture importer

HARD DEADLINE

EUDR — timber due diligence

Wooden furniture is in scope. As the first party placing goods on the EU market you are the operator: plot geolocation, proof of legal production and a documented supply chain, then a filed statement.

30 DEC 2026 · SMEs 30 JUN 2027
SIMPLIFICATION

One statement, passed downstream

Since the December 2025 simplification only the first operator files; downstream traders pass the reference number along, and filing can be annual rather than per shipment. Less paperwork — the same evidence burden behind it.

FILING BURDEN SITS WITH YOU
PRODUCT STANDARDS

EN furniture standards

Strength, durability and stability referenced to EN 1728, EN 12520 and EN 12521, with EN 1022 for seating stability. Not a customs gate, but the basis on which European retail buyers accept or reject.

CONTRACTUAL, NOT BORDER
A NOTE ON WHAT WE DON'T SELL

There is no EUDR certification scheme, and no inspection company can certify a supplier as EUDR compliant. What exists is evidence, a risk assessment and a statement that you file. We collect and verify the evidence; the statement stays yours. Treat any vendor offering an EUDR certificate with caution.

Questions

Dutch importers ask

We import into Rotterdam and sell across Europe. Who files the EUDR statement?

You do. The operator is the party that first places the goods on the EU market, and that is the importer clearing in Rotterdam — not your Indian supplier and not your German or Belgian customers. Since the December 2025 simplification only that first operator files; downstream buyers pass your reference number along. It reduces their paperwork and concentrates the evidence obligation on you.

How do we stop mould arriving on cane and rattan?

Almost entirely at origin. Natural fibre wrapped before it is fully dry will mould in a container regardless of what happens in Rotterdam. The controllable points are drying time before wrapping, moisture at packing, whether goods were packed warm, and desiccant and ventilation at stuffing — all of which are verifiable on a loading supervision, and none of which are visible in a photograph sent by the supplier.

Should we specify one moisture level for all our European customers?

Not if a meaningful share goes to centrally heated markets. We recommend 9–11% for stock staying in the Netherlands and 8–10% where furniture will sell into German or Central European homes. Buying to a single average produces mould complaints at the damp end and cracking claims at the dry end.

Can you inspect a consolidated container from several suppliers?

Yes, and on consolidated loads it matters more. One supplier's damp goods will affect the whole container. We inspect per supplier and then supervise the consolidated stuffing, so a single weak consignment is caught before it is sealed in with everything else.

Are you connected to any Indian factory or agent?

No. Qualis is buyer-paid and takes zero commission from factories. Supplier shortlisting is a fixed fee paid by you and firewalled from inspection work. An inspector paid by the party being inspected has no reason to fail anything — which is the entire reason this business exists.
