Importing furniture from India

The same container fails differently depending on where it lands. Wood that is stable in Jodhpur splits in a heated German apartment, moulds on the way to Rotterdam, and has to clear a fire regulation in Britain that exists nowhere else. These guides cover what changes by destination — written from inside India's largest wooden furniture export cluster.

THE PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE

Shipping moisture alone ranges from 6% for Canada to 11% for humid Australia and the Netherlands. If you distribute into more than one market on a single averaged specification, you will get mould complaints at the damp end and cracking claims at the dry end. Specify per destination, not per supplier.

Common to every route

What goes wrong regardless of destination

Four failures we see on India-origin furniture no matter who the buyer is or where the container lands.

RISK 01 · TIMBER

Moisture nobody measured

Kiln cycles get skipped when orders run late. The furniture leaves India looking perfect and fails weeks after delivery, which is why it reaches you as a customer return rather than a shipping dispute.

RISK 02 · COUNTERPARTY

A trader presenting as a factory

Our most common verification finding. A real, registered business with no machinery and no control over whoever actually builds your order — so quality problems become unfixable.

RISK 03 · SUBCONTRACTING

The sample and the run diverge

An approved sample built by the owner's best two men, then production spread across three workshops. Nobody lied, and nothing matches.

RISK 04 · QUANTITY

Counted against the wrong document

Cartons reconciled to the supplier's packing list rather than your invoice. Discovered at destination, it is a credit-note negotiation with a supplier who has already been paid.
