Importing furniture from India to the USA

American interiors are the driest of any market we ship to, and American paperwork is the heaviest. Wood has to leave India at a moisture content most Indian workshops never hit, and every container needs species declarations, formaldehyde certification and — for dressers and upholstery — test reports that trace to the batch actually built.

The physical risk

The driest destination we ship to

Forced-air heating through a northern winter takes interiors drier than a British or German home. Furniture built to a Rajasthan workshop's moisture content does not survive its first January in Chicago or Denver.

WHY THIS MATTERS MORE IN THE US THAN ANYWHERE

American consumers return furniture. A cracked tabletop that a European buyer might negotiate over becomes an Amazon or Wayfair return, a one-star review, and in a marketplace model a chargeback against you. The financial consequence of shipping wet wood is larger here than in any other market on our list.

HOW WE CHECK — multiple pin-meter readings per item across tops, panels and rails, recorded individually rather than averaged, photographed with the meter in frame, and failed against the destination specification rather than a generic number.

Regulatory position

Five frameworks, and the paperwork now files electronically

The US does not have EUDR, but it has a documentation load that catches importers who assume otherwise. Paper Lacey Act declarations ended on 1 January 2026 — filings now go through CBP's ACE system or the APHIS Lacey Act web service.

US FURNITURE IMPORT — WHAT APPLIES TO INDIAN WOOD FURNITURE
LACEY ACTPPQ Form 505 species declaration — every shipment. Scientific name, country of harvest and quantity for every wood species in the product, including MDF and particleboard components. Phase VII enforcement from 1 December 2024 brought the remaining HTS 9403 furniture categories into scope. Civil penalties reach $10,000 per violation, with criminal exposure up to $500,000 for knowing violations and forfeiture of the cargo.
TSCA TITLE VIFormaldehyde limits on composite wood. Hardwood plywood at or below 0.05 ppm, particleboard 0.09, MDF 0.11, thin MDF 0.13 — certified by an EPA-recognised third-party certifier and labelled as TSCA Title VI compliant. Importers must hold the supplier's certification before import; non-compliant goods can be detained at the border.
STURDY / F2057Tip-over testing for clothing storage units. Dressers and chests need ASTM F2057-23 test reports under the STURDY Act. This is a design-and-build requirement, not a paperwork one — a unit that fails cannot be made compliant after it is built.
16 CFR 1640Upholstery flammability. Upholstered seating must meet the TB 117-2013 smoulder resistance requirements, with the associated label. Same substitution risk as the UK: the certificate is real, the foam in the container may not be the foam that was tested.
CPSIAChildren's furniture. Anything intended for children needs a Children's Product Certificate based on testing at a CPSC-accepted laboratory, covering lead and the applicable juvenile product standards.
CITESCheck your Dalbergia position. Sheesham is a Dalbergia species, and CITES annotations covering that genus have been amended more than once. Confirm the current annotation and any finished-product exemption with your broker before you ship — this is the one that surprises first-time sheesham importers.
WHERE THIS BREAKS IN PRACTICE

Not at the filing. At the source. A Lacey declaration needs a scientific name and a country of harvest, and a Jodhpur factory buying through timber yards frequently cannot produce either with confidence. A TSCA certificate is only as good as the panel actually used in your batch. Both are verifiable on site — and only on site.

Not legal advice. An orientation summary for US furniture importers, not a compliance opinion. Requirements differ by product, material and end use, tariff treatment changes frequently, and EPA proposed updates to the formaldehyde rule in February 2026. Confirm your position with a licensed customs broker and qualified counsel against current CBP, EPA, APHIS and CPSC guidance.

Where US orders go wrong

Four failures on the India–US route

RISK 01 · TIMBER

Wet wood into dry homes

The largest claim category on US-bound orders. Sheesham shipped above 10% shrinks hard in a forced-air heated interior, and the splits surface four to eight weeks after delivery — as returns, not shipping disputes.

RISK 02 · TRANSIT DAMAGE

Packaging built for a shorter journey

US e-commerce means parcel and LTL handling after a six-week sea leg. Cartons that survive an Indian domestic trip fail six-orientation drop testing, and a high return rate is almost always a packaging problem rather than a product one.

RISK 03 · SUBSTITUTION

Certified components swapped mid-run

Certified panel or foam runs out, cheaper stock goes in, and the certificate on file no longer describes what is in the container. Only catchable by someone standing in the factory during production.

RISK 04 · DOCUMENTATION

Species nobody can actually name

A Lacey declaration needs a genus and species and a country of harvest. Timber bought through yards often arrives with a purchase invoice and a common name — which is not the same thing.

Questions

US importers ask

What moisture content should I specify for the US market?

7–9% for solid hardwood, verified with multiple readings per item rather than a batch average. That is tighter than we recommend for the UK or Germany, because forced-air heating in the northern US takes interiors drier than European homes. If your volume goes primarily to Florida or the Gulf Coast the risk inverts and we would specify differently — tell us the destination region, not just the country.

Can you verify Lacey Act species information at the factory?

We can verify what is verifiable and tell you plainly what is not. We check the species declared against what is physically in the batch, trace the timber back through yards and traders as far as the chain genuinely goes, and photograph the purchase and transit documents. What we cannot do is invent a country of harvest that the supply chain never recorded — and knowing that before you file is the point.

Do you check TSCA Title VI compliance?

We trace the certification to the actual panel in production, which is where it usually breaks. Certified stock runs out mid-run, cheaper board goes in, and the certificate on file no longer describes the container. We are not a testing laboratory — emissions testing is done by an EPA-recognised third-party certifier — but we can confirm whether the certificates are current, genuine and matched to your goods.

We sell on Amazon and Wayfair. What should we prioritise?

Packaging and carton data before anything else. Most marketplace losses on Indian furniture are transit damage and routing-guide chargebacks rather than product defects. ISTA 3A drop testing on the real production packaging, plus verified carton dimensions, weights and markings, will move your return rate more than any additional quality check.

How quickly can you get to a factory?

Same day in Jodhpur and Jaipur, 24 hours in Delhi NCR, 48–72 hours in Saharanpur, Moradabad and Kolkata. Bookings confirmed within two hours with a fixed quote. India is 9.5 to 12.5 hours ahead of US time zones, so a request sent at the end of your working day is usually answered before you start the next one.

Are you connected to any Indian factory or agent?

No. Qualis is buyer-paid and takes zero commission from factories. Supplier shortlisting is a fixed fee paid by you and firewalled from inspection work. An inspector paid by the party being inspected has no reason to fail anything.
