Importing furniture from India to Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia runs the strictest conformity regime in the region. If the SABER certificates are not registered before your container reaches Jeddah or Dammam, the shipment does not wait for paperwork — it re-exports at your expense. On a sailing measured in days, whatever is wrong when the doors close is wrong when it lands. Here is what to control before the goods leave India.

The Saudi-specific gate

SABER is the border, not a formality

Saudi Arabia clears regulated goods against certificates registered before arrival — not documentation assembled afterwards. Wood furniture, upholstery and lighting sit in regulated territory, and the certificate work starts with you as the importer, not with your Indian supplier.

WHO HOLDS THE SABER CERTIFICATE
ORIGINIndian factory. Supplies the technical file and test reports against the applicable SASO standards. It does not hold the certificate.
YOUSaudi importer. Register the product (PCoC) and each shipment (SCoC) in SABER, hold the certificates, and carry the exposure at customs.
CUSTOMSJeddah or Dammam. Clears the container only against certificates already registered in SABER. Nothing is settled after arrival.
THE RULE THAT CATCHES PEOPLE

Letters of undertaking that once let shipments enter pending paperwork are no longer accepted, and a certificate obtained after arrival is treated as non-compliance. If goods reach Jeddah or Dammam without the required documentation registered in SABER, the consequence is re-export — the shipment returns at the importer's expense. Compliance has to be complete before shipment, not corrected after it.

THE SAUDI CONFORMITY STACK
SALEEMThe product safety programme. Saudi's framework for product conformity, operated through the SABER platform.
SASOStandards, Metrology & Quality Org. Sets the technical regulations and standards your furniture is judged against, and the Arabic labelling requirements.
SABER · PCoCProduct Certificate of Conformity. A product-level certificate issued through an approved conformity body and registered by you as importer.
SABER · SCoCShipment Certificate of Conformity. Issued per consignment, and required before the goods reach Saudi customs.
ONE THING THAT DOES NOT APPLY HERE

There is no EUDR, Lacey Act or UK-style timber due diligence regime in Saudi Arabia. If you sell only within the Kingdom, the deforestation paperwork that consumes European and American importers is not your problem — SABER conformity is. It becomes your problem the moment you also supply an EU or UK customer, and if that is on your roadmap, build the timber evidence from the first order rather than retrofitting it later.

The physical risk

Furniture lives between 50°C and an air conditioner

Saudi furniture experiences a swing nothing temperate does. Desert heat and, on the coast, humidity outside; aggressively conditioned dry air inside; and often a spell in an unconditioned warehouse in between. Wood moves through all three, and inland Riyadh is drier than almost anywhere it was made.

THE DIFFERENCE FROM EUROPE

European furniture gets a dry season and a recovery season. Saudi furniture does not — conditioned air keeps it dry all year, so shrinkage never reverses. Wood that would survive a German winter and re-equilibrate by June simply keeps moving here. It is also why adhesive and veneer quality matter more in this market than joint strength alone.

FF&E 02 · CONSISTENCY

Two hundred chairs, three workshops

Volume orders get subcontracted. Finish and dimension drift between workshops is invisible in photographs and glaring across a hotel restaurant floor.

FF&E 03 · FIRE

Contract upholstery requirements

Furnishings in public and commercial premises carry fire-performance expectations under Saudi civil-defence and high-commission safety requirements. Confirm the applicable specification with your consultant before production, not at delivery.

FF&E 04 · CALENDAR

Two calendars, one deadline

Indian production stops for Diwali and regional festivals; Saudi approvals and logistics slow through Ramadan, Eid and the Hajj season. Both are predictable and both get left out of schedules.

Questions

Saudi importers ask

Can you issue our SASO or SABER certificate?

No, and be careful with anyone who says they can while also selling you inspection. Only bodies authorised by SASO may issue product or shipment certificates, and SABER registration sits with you as the importer. We work alongside your conformity assessment body: they certify the product, we verify that what is actually in your container matches what was certified. Those are different jobs and it is healthier that different firms do them.

We import directly into Saudi Arabia. What is the real SABER risk?

Arriving without the documentation registered in SABER. Letters of undertaking are no longer accepted and a certificate obtained after arrival counts as non-compliance, with re-export at your expense as the consequence rather than a fine or a delay. Wood furniture, upholstery and lighting sit in regulated territory, so the certificate work has to be finished before the goods sail.

Does furniture imported to Saudi Arabia need Arabic labelling?

Yes. SASO requirements expect product information, markings and safety and care details in Arabic, and clearance and retail listing both depend on it. Because labels and inserts are printed at the factory, a missing or English-only label is a reprint in Jodhpur if we catch it at inspection — or a clearance problem if it is caught in Jeddah.

What moisture content should we specify?

8–10% for solid hardwood. Air conditioning keeps Saudi interiors dry year-round rather than seasonally, so shrinkage continues instead of reversing in summer, and inland Riyadh is drier still. We also weight adhesive, veneer and edge-banding checks more heavily here, since heat during transit and unconditioned storage attacks those before it attacks joints.

Do EUDR or timber legality rules apply to us?

Not for goods sold within Saudi Arabia — there is no equivalent regime here; SABER conformity is the gate instead. Timber due diligence applies the moment you supply an EU or UK customer, and retrofitting that evidence onto an existing supplier is far harder than building it from the first order.

How fast can you get to a factory?

Same day in Jodhpur and Jaipur, 24 hours in Delhi NCR, 48–72 hours in Saharanpur, Moradabad and Kolkata. Bookings confirmed within two hours. Saudi time is two and a half hours behind us, so we work essentially the same day as you — which matters when a loading date moves at short notice.
